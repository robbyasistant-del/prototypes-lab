Gravity Garden - Full Strategy Report

This report documents the design, systems, progression, and implementation choices behind the Gravity Garden HTML5 prototype.

1. Game Summary

Gravity Garden is a cosmic placement-strategy game. The player plants a limited number of gravity seeds in the lower garden. These seeds grow into gravity wells that attract falling star-seeds. When stars enter a well, they bloom into luminous stellar flowers, creating score and occasionally chain reactions. The feel is meditative, but the strategy is tight: every planted seed matters.

2. Core Mechanics

Planting Phase

The player can only plant on the bottom “ground” region. This anchors strategy to a readable spatial rule and creates meaningful placement tradeoffs.

Gravity Wells

Each planted seed grows into a well with animated radius and simplified inverse-square attraction. Multiple wells can combine to create star funnels.

Blooming

Stars convert into flowers when they enter a well’s core. Each bloom emits particles and cosmic pollen.

Chain Reactions

If 3 or more blooms happen inside a 2-second window, the chain multiplier rises and a supernova event triggers bonus score plus spectacle.

3. Star Types

  • White stars: Standard targets and baseline scoring.
  • Gold stars: Worth 2x score, helping advanced players recover a shaky run.
  • Comets: Faster and harder to bend, but valuable when captured.

4. Level Progression

The prototype includes 6 levels with increasing pressure:

  • Higher star spawn rates
  • Stricter seed budgets
  • Growing wind intensity
  • More floating obstacles that deflect stars
  • Higher comet frequency

Win: Reach the target bloom count.
Lose: Run out of seeds and fail to hit target after all stars resolve.

5. Scoring Model

Score is driven by this structure:

  • Base bloom score = 100
  • Total = Base × chain multiplier × star-type bonus
  • Supernova adds a large flat bonus
  • Pollen interactions add small trickle score for satisfying after-effects

This creates a strong risk-reward loop: safe isolated catches are steady, but clustered catches create explosive scoring.

6. Wind, Obstacles, and Pollen

  • Wind gusts periodically push stars laterally, forcing adaptive layouts.
  • Obstacles bounce stars and make late levels less deterministic.
  • Cosmic pollen drifts from flowers and energizes nearby blooms, making the garden feel alive even after a catch.

7. Badges

The game includes 5 unlockable celestial badges to support replay motivation:

  • First Bloom
  • Supernova
  • Golden Hand
  • Level Keeper
  • Celestial Zenith

8. Visual Direction

The visual target was “cosmic greenhouse” rather than generic sci-fi. The presentation combines:

  • Layered nebula gradients
  • Glowing wells with soft radial falloff
  • Star trails for motion readability
  • Flower petals with hue-shifted gradients
  • Particle bursts for blooms and supernovas
  • Glassmorphism UI to keep HUD readable without flattening the atmosphere

9. Technical Notes

  • Single self-contained index.html
  • Canvas-based rendering and animation loop
  • Simplified inverse-square gravity model
  • Touch + mouse support
  • Responsive HUD and readable mobile interaction
  • Designed for smooth browser performance at 60fps on typical hardware

10. Strategy Tips for Players

  • Use early seeds to create broad catch coverage.
  • Place at least one center-biased well to recover wind-drifted stars.
  • In later levels, cluster one or two wells closer together to fish for chain reactions.
  • Do not over-commit all seeds to one side unless wind strongly favors it.
  • Comets are hard to catch directly, so use overlapping pull fields.
